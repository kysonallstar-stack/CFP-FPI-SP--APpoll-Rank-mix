Where humans and computers disagree

Poll likes them more

    Computers like them more

      How it works

      Rating. SP+ and ESPN's FPI are converted to z-scores and averaged (the "computers"). That is blended with the AP poll's vote points (the "humans"). The poll's weight grows from 25% in week 5 to 40% by week 12. The result is shown in points, so the difference between two teams is a predicted margin before home field.

      Playoff selection. Once the committee releases its rankings, they replace the AP poll at a 50% weight, but only for predicting who the committee picks. Game predictions keep using the AP.

      Odds. Every remaining game, each conference title game and the 12-team selection are simulated 10,000 times. Home field is worth 2.7 points. Each simulated season also draws how much better or worse each team really is than its rating, so a team's results move together instead of every game being a separate coin flip.

      The committee is approximated. In each simulation it ranks teams by rating, subtracts points for every loss and adds a little for conference titles. Those weights were fitted to the committee's actual 2024 and 2025 final rankings. The real committee weighs many things this ignores.

      Known weaknesses

      • Early-season priors. SP+ and FPI lean on preseason expectations for the first few weeks.
      • Few games between conferences. Conferences are compared mostly through a handful of non-conference games.
      • The committee model is a simple approximation (rating, losses, titles).
      • Injuries aren't modeled, including a starting quarterback going down.
      • Tiebreakers are simplified: record among the tied teams, then rating.
